# Practice, assessment, and review

## Conversational assignments

Choose a communication goal, setting, roles, facts each participant knows, and a natural stopping point. State necessary background and vocabulary boundaries without supplying the learner's response. The tutor needs enough facts to answer coherently; do not change hidden details merely to make the learner fail.

Useful forms include:

- Role-play with one plausible complication, such as an unavailable item.
- Information gap: learner questions reveal directions, opening times, or preferences.
- Short personal account with relevant follow-up questions. Allow invented details; do not require sensitive disclosures.
- Agreement or negotiation: choose a suitable option from different preferences.
- Listening and response: hear a short exchange and act on it without seeing the transcript first, when real audio is available.
- Repair: ask for repetition, confirm meaning, paraphrase around a missing word.
- Retelling or delayed reuse in a new context, with complexity appropriate to ability.

Distinguish reading from listening: a visible transcript, subtitles, or written phrase prompt may make an attempt supported rather than independent listening. Save conditions actually used. Repetition after a model is valuable practice but does not demonstrate spontaneous production.

## Correction and support

Coaching mode can explain, model, and invite another attempt. Conversation mode prioritizes the ongoing exchange; give a short debrief with useful corrections, a natural version, and an opportunity to retry. Avoid a long catalogue of errors. Respect requests for more or less correction.

Treat valid regional and register differences as variation. Aim for understandable, socially appropriate speech rather than accent elimination. Correct pronunciation only from accessible audio evidence; confirm uncertain recognition instead of assuming every transcription error was the learner's error. Avoid precise phonetic or timing claims when the signal does not support them.

## Assessment packet

Before the first assessed turn, save an immutable `assessment` record with:

- Lesson/course scope, approved plan ID, target ability IDs, source references for tested usage.
- Scenario, roles, supplied facts, allowed supports, required modalities, and a bounded ending.
- Prepared opening and eligible branches/complications; natural responses within these branches are allowed. Do not force a fixed script or improvise extra rescue tasks after weak performance.
- Required success criteria and acceptable variation. Keep role secrets and model responses out of the learner-facing prompt.

Announce a short assessment scope and deliver one turn at a time. Save actual exchanges and the exact pending turn at natural boundaries. Each delivery/attempt has its own ID, even when sharing a frozen packet. A switch preserves the old pending turn and never routes an answer into a different assessment implicitly.

## Assess communication

Set criteria proportionate to the lesson. Typical dimensions:

| Dimension | Evidence |
| --- | --- |
| Goal | Learner obtains, conveys, or agrees the necessary information. |
| Understanding | Learner responds to the actual meaning of replies, including a reasonable variation. |
| Production/interaction | Learner forms appropriate responses beyond copying the model. |
| Repair | Learner resolves a planned misunderstanding or missing information when required. |
| Intelligibility | Meaning can be understood from accessible speech; assess only with audio evidence. |
| Social use | Required register/politeness suits this situation and variety. |

Use qualitative evidence and explicit criteria by default. If numerical scoring is requested, agree a rubric before the attempt; do not present it as a standardized proficiency measure. Do not import the Source-Grounded Tutor's 33% floor into language performance.

An activity can pass with minor errors if it meets all required communicative criteria independently under the declared support conditions. If required criteria are observed and missed, use `not_yet_passed`. If required evidence cannot be obtained, use `incomplete_evidence`, identify missing dimensions, and preserve whatever was demonstrated. Do not award a spoken/listening pass from text alone.

Record hints, translations, supplied phrases, or revealed answers as assistance for affected criteria. Neutral task clarification is not assistance. Do not coach through an assessment and retroactively count the corrected response as independent. Stop at the prepared end; debrief and offer focused practice afterward. A new assessment attempt is append-only.

Keep packet, attempt, result, and ability status separate. `Done` closes teaching only; the assessment result determines demonstrated evidence. A completed lesson does not by itself establish mastery or retention.

## Review and course finish

Review a few high-value recurring difficulties. Record the target phrase/pattern, supporting evidence, next activity, and a due date or future lesson trigger. Review dates are course data, not external reminders or automations. Do not create calendar events or notifications without a request.

Track ability judgments with evidence:

- `introduced`: explained or encountered.
- `practising`: attempted, still supported or unreliable.
- `demonstrated`: achieved independently in a fresh situation under agreed conditions.
- `retained`: independently reused later after delay or genuine intervening work.
- `unassessed`: required evidence is unavailable or has not been gathered.

Track modalities/dimensions separately when needed: an ability can have demonstrated wording and unassessed listening. Later evidence may change the current judgment; preserve earlier judgments and link the new one with `supersedes` rather than rewriting them.

For the cumulative assessment, prepare fresh scenarios combining required course goals and sample all required abilities in suitable modality. Review gaps afterward. If postponed/declined, record the learner's decision and describe completion accurately. Do not infer general fluency, CEFR certification, or command of all situations beyond this course.
